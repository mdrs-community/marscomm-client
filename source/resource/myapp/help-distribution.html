Chat & Distribution

MarsComm's chat system lets crew members and Mission Control exchange text messages subject to a communications delay. Every message is addressed to a specific set of people — its distribution. Only users on that distribution can see the message.

The Communications Delay

All messages travel with a one-way delay, simulating the speed-of-light lag between Earth and Mars. When you send a message to someone on the other planet, they will not see it until the delay has elapsed. Likewise, their reply will take the same amount of time to reach you. Plan for round-trip times of twice the one-way delay. MarsComm supports both realistic Mars delays and delays that are shorter or longer. The delay used is chosen by Mission Control in alignment with mission objectives.

Messages sent to someone on the same planet as you arrive instantly, with no delay.

While a message is in transit, a circular progress indicator is shown to the sender and to others on the same planet who have visibiilty of that message. When the message arrives on the other side, a soft chime plays.

What is a Distribution?

The distribution is the list of people who are part of a conversation. Think of it like the "To:" field on an email — only the people listed can see the messages. The sender is always part of the distribution.

All messages that share the same distribution belong to the same Chat. If you send a message to the Commander and Engineer, and later send another message to the same two people, both messages appear in the same Chat thread. Note, however, that if someone else sends a message to the same two people (Commander and Engineer in this example), it will be in a different Chat because the sender is implicitly included on the Chat.

Setting the Distribution

Use the checkboxes in the Distribution panel to choose who your next message will go to. Mission Control users are listed on the left, crew members on the right. You do not need to check your own name — you are always included automatically.

The Group dropdown above the checkboxes provides convenient presets for the Distribution:

  • All — selects everyone (crew and Mission Control together)
  • Mission Control — selects all Earth-side staff
  • Crew — selects all Mars-side crew members
  • Any custom groups defined for this mission (e.g. "Command", "Medical")
  • Custom — set automatically when you check/uncheck boxes manually

After you change the distribution, wait a moment — the chat panel will update to show the conversation thread for your selected group. If no conversation exists yet with that exact set of people, the panel will be empty and your next message will start a new thread.

The Chat List

The Chat list (the column on the left of the Distribution panel) shows all existing conversations for the current Sol that include you. Click any item to switch to that conversation and update the checkboxes to match its distribution.

Chat names are generated automatically:

  • If the distribution matches a named group (All, Mission Control, Crew, or a custom group), the chat is named after that group.
  • If the conversation is with exactly one other person, the chat is named after their role.
  • Otherwise, the chat is named by a short list of role abbreviations, e.g. C, EO, MCD. Hover over the name to see the full list of roles.

When a new message arrives in a conversation you are not currently viewing, that conversation's name turns bold red in the Chat list. Clicking it will clear the unread indicator and show the messages.

Distribution vs. Group vs. Chat

While Distribution, Group, Chat are closely related concepts, they are not the same things. Remember:

  • A distribution is just a set of users that will get a message.
  • A group is just a convenient way of choosing a distribution.
  • When a message is sent to a distribution, a Chat is automatically created unless one with the exact same distribtion already exists. It is within a Chat that messages live.
This system makes it very easy and convenient to send messages to exactly who you want, as Chats are created automatically as needed.

Sending a Message

Type your message in the text box at the bottom of the chat panel and press Send (or hit Enter). The message will be added to the current Chat immediately on your side, and will arrive on the other planet after the communications delay.

Basic text formatting is supported:

  • **bold**
  • __italic__
  • `code`
  • Emoticons: :) and :(
  • URLs are automatically turned into clickable links

Emoji Reactions

Each message has a small row of emoji buttons to its right: 👍 😊 😉 😞. Clicking one sends that emoji as a reaction to that specific message. Reactions appear as a small annotation line below the message rather than as a separate chat entry, keeping the conversation thread readable.

You can also click the emoji buttons in the row above the message input to insert that emoji into your message text.

Replies

Click the ↩ button next to any message to enter reply mode. A strip appears above the input box showing a snippet of the message you are replying to. Your next sent message will be visually linked to the original, showing the quoted snippet. Click the ✕ in the strip to cancel and return to normal messaging.

In the chat thread, reply headers are clickable — clicking one will scroll to and briefly highlight the original message being replied to.

Editing a Sent Message

Press the up-arrow key in an empty message input to recall your most recently sent message for editing. Make your changes and press Update to replace the original. The edited version is delivered to recipients after the usual communications delay, and shows an "(edited)" marker.

Navigating Past Sols

Use the Sol spinner in the top bar to view chats from previous Sols. When viewing a past Sol, the message input is disabled — you cannot send messages into the past. The Chat list and messages are read-only for historical Sols.

Remember: The person on the other planet has no idea you sent a message until the delay has elapsed. There is no way to retract a message once sent. Read your message carefully before sending.
Tip: For private conversations within Mission Control or within the crew, select only the relevant people using the checkboxes before sending. This keeps the shared "All" thread focused on mission-wide communications.
